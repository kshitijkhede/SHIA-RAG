[L108] #### 2.1.1 Flat-Chunk RAG Systems
[L110] The foundational RAG approach was introduced by Lewis et al. (2020), combining dense passage retrieval

[L112] #### 2.1.2 Structured RAG Systems
[L114] Recent work has begun exploring structured retrieval:
[L116] | System | Approach | Key Limitation |
[L117] |--------|----------|----------------|
[L118] | RAPTOR (Sarthi et al., 2024) | Recursive abstractive processing — clusters chunks and generates

[L119] | GraphRAG (Edge et al., 2024) | Builds a knowledge graph from text, retrieves subgraphs | No hierarchy;

[L120] | TreeRAG (Concept) | Organizes documents into topic trees for retrieval | No formal hierarchy induction

[L121] | HiChunk (LlamaIndex) | Hierarchical chunking with parent-child relationships between chunks | Chunks

[L123] #### 2.1.3 Knowledge Graph Construction
[L125] Ontology learning and taxonomy induction have a rich history in NLP:
[L126] - Hearst patterns (Hearst, 1992) extract hypernym relations from text

[L389] ### 1.2 Proposed Solution: SHIA-RAG
[L390]
[L391] SHIA-RAG (Semantic Hierarchy Induction Architecture for Retrieval-Augmented Generation) addresses all
five limitations by replacing flat text chunks with a structured Knowledge Forest:
[L392]
[L393]
[L394] Traditional RAG:
[L395] SHIA-RAG:

Document → [Chunk₁, Chunk₂, ..., Chunkₙ] → Top-k → LLM
Document → Knowledge Forest (concepts + relations) → Adaptive Traversal → LLM

[L396]

[L397]
[L398] The system:
[L399] 1. Extracts knowledge — concepts, definitions, relations, and evidence from raw documents
[L400] 2. Builds a Knowledge Forest — a hierarchy of semantically linked concept nodes organized as rooted
trees
[L401] 3. Retrieves knowledge nodes — by traversing the hierarchy (parents, children, cross-links) with adaptive
depth
[L402] 4. Selects context optimally — using a Tree Knapsack algorithm that respects hierarchical precedence
constraints
[L403] 5. Generates verified answers — with claim-level evidence verification and citation binding
[L404] 6. Learns from feedback — a self-evolution loop that updates edge weights based on retrieval
success/failure

Operating Systems → Scheduling → Round Robin"), not by arbitrary text windows. SHIA-RAG mirrors this natural
organization for AI retrieval.

[L536] ### 2.1 Related Work
[L537]
[L538] #### 2.1.1 Flat-Chunk RAG Systems
[L539]
[L540] The foundational RAG approach was introduced by Lewis et al. (2020), combining dense passage retrieval
with seq2seq generation. Subsequent systems — REALM (Guu et al., 2020), RETRO (Borgeaud et al., 2022),
Atlas (Izacard et al., 2023), and Self-RAG (Asai et al., 2024) — improved retrieval quality, generation
faithfulness, and self-reflection, but all operate on flat text chunks with no structural awareness.
[L541]
[L542] #### 2.1.2 Structured RAG Systems
[L543]
[L544] Recent work has begun exploring structured retrieval:
[L545]
[L546] | System | Approach | Key Limitation |
[L547] |--------|----------|----------------|
[L548] | RAPTOR (Sarthi et al., 2024) | Recursive abstractive processing — clusters chunks and generates
summaries at multiple levels | Top-down summaries only; no concept-level extraction or entity resolution |
[L549] | GraphRAG (Edge et al., 2024) | Builds a knowledge graph from text, retrieves subgraphs | No hierarchy;
flat graph structure; no self-evolution |
[L550] | TreeRAG (Concept) | Organizes documents into topic trees for retrieval | No formal hierarchy induction
algorithm; manual tree construction |
[L551] | HiChunk (LlamaIndex) | Hierarchical chunking with parent-child relationships between chunks | Chunks
are still text fragments, not semantic concepts |
[L552]
[L553] #### 2.1.3 Knowledge Graph Construction
[L554]
[L555] Ontology learning and taxonomy induction have a rich history in NLP:
[L556] - Hearst patterns (Hearst, 1992) extract hypernym relations from text
[L557] - Taxonomy induction (Velardi et al., 2013; Bansal et al., 2014) builds hierarchies from web corpora
[L558] - Knowledge Graph Embedding (TransE, RotatE) learns vector representations of entities and relations
[L559]
[L560] However, none of these systems integrate hierarchy construction with RAG retrieval and self-

[L533] ## 1. Project Overview
[L534]
[L535] ### 1.1 What is SHIA-RAG?
[L536]
[L537] SHIA-RAG (Semantic Hierarchy Induction Architecture for Retrieval-Augmented Generation) is a nextgeneration RAG system that fundamentally changes how information is retrieved for LLM-based question
answering.
[L538]
[L539] Traditional RAG splits documents into flat text chunks → embeds them → retrieves top-k similar chunks
→ feeds to LLM.

[L540]
[L541] SHIA-RAG instead:
[L542] 1. Extracts knowledge (concepts, definitions, relations) from documents
[L543] 2. Builds a Knowledge Forest — a hierarchy of semantically linked concept nodes (not text chunks)
[L544] 3. Retrieves knowledge nodes by traversing the hierarchy (parents, children, siblings)
[L545] 4. Assembles context intelligently within a token budget
[L546] 5. Generates answers with citation-backed evidence
[L547]
[L548] ### 1.2 Why It Matters
[L549]
[L550] | Problem with Traditional RAG | How SHIA-RAG Solves It |
[L551] |---|---|
[L552] | Chunks break semantic continuity | Knowledge nodes preserve complete concepts |
[L553] | Duplicate/fragmented information | Entity resolution merges synonyms into single nodes |
[L554] | Fixed chunk granularity | Hierarchy allows multi-level retrieval (broad → specific) |
[L555] | Weak multi-hop reasoning | Parent-child paths encode reasoning chains |
[L556] | No cross-document fusion | Knowledge Forest merges concepts across all documents |
[L557]
[L558] ### 1.3 Core Innovation
[L559]
[L560] > Key Insight: Humans organize knowledge by topics and subtopics (e.g., "CS → OS → Scheduling →
Round Robin"), not by arbitrary text windows. SHIA-RAG mirrors this natural organization for AI retrieval.

[L684]
[L685] What exists: TreeRAG and RAPTOR use simple clustering. GraphRAG uses flat triple extraction.

[L687] What SHIA-RAG proposes: Hierarchy construction as a formal energy minimization problem:
[L688]
[L689] $$J(\mathcal{F}) = \sum_{(P_i, N_i)} \left[ \lambda_1 \cdot L_{\text{sem}}(P_i, N_i) + \lambda_2 \cdot
L_{\text{depth}}(N_i) + \lambda_3 \cdot L_{\text{conf}}(P_i, N_i) + \lambda_4 \cdot L_{\text{onto}}(P_i, N_i)
\right]$$
[L690]
[L691] Where:
[L692] - $L_{\text{sem}}$ = semantic distance loss (1 - cosine similarity)
[L693] - $L_{\text{depth}}$ = depth regularization (penalizes too-deep or too-shallow trees)
[L694] - $L_{\text{conf}}$ = confidence loss (low-confidence placements increase energy)
[L695] - $L_{\text{onto}}$ = ontological consistency loss (parent should be more general)
[L696]
[L697] Why novel: No RAG paper has formalized hierarchy construction as a multi-objective optimization
problem for hierarchy induction in RAG. The formulation enables principled trade-offs between semantic fit,
structural balance, confidence, and ontological consistency.
[L698]
[L699] > [!NOTE]
[L700] > The $L_{\text{onto}}$ term uses a sigmoid relaxation $\sigma(\text{type\_score}(P) - \text{type\_score}
(N))$ instead of a non-differentiable indicator function. See Section 8.1 for the corrected formulation.
